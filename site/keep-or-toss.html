Every piece of paper gets the same four questions, in order. This page walks through them.

The tree

Start with any document in your hand and follow the questions top to bottom:

START: You are holding a piece of paper.
│
├─ Q1: Would it cost me real money or real trouble
│      if I threw this away and needed it later?
│      │
│      ├─ YES ────────────────────────────────────┐
│      │                                          ▼
│      │                              Q2: Does anyone but me ever
│      │                              need the ORIGINALS of this?
│      │                              (wet-ink signatures, passports,
│      │                               birth/death certificates, deeds,
│      │                               some government forms)
│      │                                 │
│      ├─ NO ──┐                          ├─ YES → KEEP the paper in one
│      │      │                          │         file box. Scan it too.
│      │      │                          └─ NO  → SCAN it, keep the file,
│      │      │                                   RECYCLE the paper.
│      │      ▼
│      │  Q3: Is it 2+ years old and just a record of
│      │  a moment that has passed?
│      │  (old utility bill, expired coupon, receipt
│      │   for something unpurchasable now)
│      │    │
│      │    ├─ NO  → It probably belongs in one of the
│      │    │        digital folders. Scan or file it.
│      │    └─ YES → RECYCLE it. (Shred if it has your
│      │              account numbers or SSN on it.)
│      │
└─ Q4: Is it sentimental? (kids' drawings, cards, photos)
       │
       └─ Yes → RULE: keep it only if it still makes you

                A box of every drawing does not. No absence of
                guilt in this handbook.

(Yes, Q4 lives slightly outside the main flow. Sentimental clutter refuses to follow anyone's tree. That's normal.)

The three homes a document can live in

1. In a drawer (paper originals you must keep). Trust documents, property records, government certificates, passports, social security cards. One box, one label. That's your whole "must be paper" filing system.

2. In your digital folders (everything else worth keeping). Statements, bills, insurance policies, tax records, warranties, manuals, receipts. Scanned or saved as PDF.

3. In the shredder or recycling (everything else). Most paper belongs here eventually. The retention table tells you when.

Keep digital, keep paper — at a glance

Document Keep digital? Keep paper?
Monthly utility bill Yes, until the next one arrives and the payment cleared No
Tax return + support Yes, at least 7 years Optional (digital copies are widely accepted)
Insurance policies Yes Yes, until renewed/replaced
Mortgage or deed Yes Yes (originals matter)
Car title Yes Yes (originals required to sell)
Passport / birth certificate Scan for reference only Yes
Warranties and receipts Yes, in the purchase's folder No
Owners' manuals Usually available online — keep the PDF No
Kids' art and schoolwork Photograph the best pieces Handful, rotated yearly
Greeting cards No Only ones that still mean something
"Records of you paying online" The website already has them No
Anything with an account number you'll cancel anyway No No — shred or recycle

The two-line summary

  • Trouble if lost, and originals matter? → Keep the paper (and scan a backup).
  • Trouble if lost, originals don't matter? → Scan it, keep the file, recycle the paper.
  • No trouble if lost? → Recycle (shred first if it has account numbers or an SSN).
