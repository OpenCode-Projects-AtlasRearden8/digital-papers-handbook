Once a year, sixty minutes, and the whole system stays healthy. This is the entire maintenance plan for the handbook.

When to do it

Pick an hour on a day you're already home and mildly bored. Good anchors:

  • The week after taxes are filed — everything tax-related is fresh in your head
  • New Year's week (calendars get replaced; symbolically satisfying)
  • Right when a new year's first insurance renewal or tax form arrives (new stuff arriving = old stuff ready to move)

Put it on the calendar now. Seriously — the cleanup only works if it's scheduled; otherwise it becomes "that thing I meant to do."

The 60 minutes, minute by minute

Minutes What you do
0–10 Inbox sweep. Gather paper from mail piles, counters, the car, whatever physical or digital "paper inbox" has collected. Everything goes in one spot.
10–25 Sort the pile against the decision tree: keep-paper / scan / shred. Name and file scans immediately into the folder tree.
25–35 Tax folder pass. File the most recent tax year into 01-Taxes/<year>/ if not done. Check whether any return has passed its 7-year retention window — if so, retire it to 09-Archive or shred.
35–45 Folder audit light. Walk through 01- through 08-, one per minute: anything misnamed gets renamed, anything filed wrong gets moved. Don't read things. Just fix names.
45–55 Unfiled & Untitled sweep. Search your Papers/ folder for files that still don't match the naming rule (screenshots littering, Scan_2025..., IMG_0042) — rename or file them. Search for IMG_ and Scan to find them fast.
55–60 Backup check. Confirm the folder that holds Papers/ is syncing or backed up. Change anything you haven't. Feel great for a year.

Timer rules: the pass ends at 60 minutes even if unfinished — leftovers go in next year's pile, and that's fine. A finished 80% beats an abandoned 100%.

The five checkpoints (for people who don't want the full walkthrough)

Simplify each year to answering these questions:

  1. Is there paper lying around that should be scanned or shredded?
  2. Have any tax returns passed their 7-year retention window? Retire to archive or shred.
  3. Do all files in Papers/ match the naming rule?
  4. Is the backup actually happening?
  5. (Optional) Would a tool like the AI filer make next year easier?

If you missed a year (or three)

Do a 60-minute version now, at whatever point of the year. Sorting one multi-year pile takes exactly as long as sorting a single year's pile when you're ruthless; Keep or Toss is ruthless-friendly. Start the calendar, and this is the last multi-year pile there'll ever be.

The complete annual cost

One hour a year, plus the trickle when papers arrive (naming + filing: seconds per paper). Versus: the ancestral method of "a box that exists until a life event forces archaeology."
