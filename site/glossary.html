Plain-English definitions, arranged alphabetically. Every term here appears somewhere in the handbook.

Backup — A second copy of your files that survives even if the first copy dies. The handbook's stance: keep your Papers/ folder inside a syncing service or on an external drive, and copy it somewhere else entirely when it contains anything you couldn't bear to lose.

Cloud — Someone else's computer that you can reach over the internet. Useful as a backup and for reaching files from your phone; the handbook keeps your files local-first regardless.

Cost basis — What you paid for something (plus improvement costs). Matters when you sell property; the reason you keep home-improvement receipts in retention.

Ctrl+F — The keyboard shortcut that searches the text inside the file you're currently looking at. The handbook's most valuable skill; see Find Anything in Five Seconds.

Decision tree — A flowchart of yes/no questions that lands on one answer. See the Keep or Toss tree.

EOB (Explanation of Benefits) — An insurance document explaining what was billed, what the insurer paid, and what you owe. Keep until checked; discard (or shred) sooner than you'd guess.

Retention period — The length of time a specific kind of document should be kept before it can be destroyed. Collected wholesale in the retention table.

Local-first — The design rule that your files live on your computer by default, with syncing or cloud services used for convenience and backup — not as the only copy.

OCR (Optical Character Recognition) — Software turning a picture of text into searchable text. This is why some scanned PDFs respond to Ctrl+F and others don't; covered in Scanning.

Original (the paper kind) — The one physical copy with signatures, seals, or legal weight. Passports, birth certificates, deeds, titles. These live in your drawer permanently; everything else graduates to digital.

PDF — Portable Document Format; the file type that looks the same everywhere and is what scans become. The default file type of this entire handbook.

PII (Personally Identifiable Information) — Anything a stranger could use to be you: SSN, account numbers, signature, date of birth paired with name. Anything holding PII gets shredded, not recycled.

Scan — A picture of a document, ideally made with your phone's scan mode so it comes out as a clean, searchable PDF. See Scan With Your Phone.

Shred — Destroy paper identifiers so they can't be harvested from the recycling. Details and cheap methods in Shredding Rules.

SSN (Social Security number) — The single most dangerous-to-lose number in a US paper trail. Anything with it printed on it goes through the shredder, always.

Tax year — The calendar year your tax return covers (2024), not the year you filed it (2025). Filenames and folders here use the tax year, as in 2025-04-Tax-Return-2024.pdf.

Warranty — A promise about repairs for a purchase. Keep the receipt + warranty as one digital file in 07-Purchases, for the life of the product.
