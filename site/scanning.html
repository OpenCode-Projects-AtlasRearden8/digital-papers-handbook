You already own a scanner. It has a camera. This page shows you the two-minute version.

The 30-second method (most documents)

  1. Put the paper on a dark, plain surface (a dark tabletop, a black notebook cover).
  2. Point your phone's camera app at it, straight on (not at an angle).
  3. Make sure the corners of the paper are inside the frame and the light is even — no harsh shadow across half the page.
  4. Tap the shutter. Done.

That photo works for almost everything: reference copies, receipts, warranties, "just in case" records. You don't need special software to have a usable record.

The better method: your phone's built-in scan mode

Both major phone platforms have a scanner hiding in the Notes app:

  • iPhone: Open Notes → new note → camera icon → Scan Documents. It finds each page, flattens the perspective, brightens the paper, and saves a clean PDF.
  • Android: Open Drive → + → Scan (or the Camera's "Document" mode, depending on your version). Same idea: auto-crop, sharpen, straight to PDF.

Why bother? Because a scan gives you a multi-page PDF with text layers — searchable, stackable, and easier to find with Ctrl+F later.

Tips that make every scan better

  • Straight on, not at an angle. Tilted scans are harder to read and search.
  • Dark background. The scanner finds the paper's edges automatically.
  • Good light, no glare. Near a window is usually best; avoid your own shadow.
  • One document at a time, named right away. Naming is covered in Folders and Naming — name before you get distracted. (If you're thinking "I'll do it later": rename it now.)
  • Multi-page documents: Use the scan mode's "+" to keep adding pages to the same PDF before you save.
  • Double-sided: Scan side one, flip, scan side two, save as one PDF.

Where do the scans go?

Directly into the folder system from Folders and Naming. Strong recommendation: scan into the cloud-drive folder you already sync (OneDrive, Google Drive, iCloud) if you use one — you get backup for free. But the master copy lives in your file system either way. Local-first, remember.

If you want to get fancy (optional)

  • Announce that a flatbed scanner is overkill for document taming — the phone version is genuinely fine for 95% of cases. Family-size piles might justify a cheap document feeder later.
  • Some people automate naming and filing with their computer afterwards — see AI-Assisted Filing if that sounds appealing.
  • OCR ("making scans searchable") often happens automatically with the built-in scanning tools. If your PDF searches come up empty, skip ahead to Find Anything in Five Seconds, which explains why and what to do.

What about sensitive documents?

Passports, SSN cards, tax papers: yes, scan them (a reference copy is useful if the original is lost), but keep the copies inside well-named folders rather than emailing them around. If you use cloud sync, that's a reasonable trade for most people — but check your own comfort level. Basics in Shredding Rules apply to how you discard them later.
