The single most-asked question in paper-taming. Here are the answers, with the reasoning kept to one line each.

The core table

Document Keep for Why (one line)
Tax returns (the return itself) 7 years minimum IRS generally has 3 years to audit, 6 for substantial underreporting; 7 is a comfortable cushion
Tax support documents (W-2s, 1099s, receipts for deductions) 7 years, with the return they belong to They prove the return's numbers
Property tax records / improvement receipts Whole time you own the property + 7 years Improvements adjust your cost basis when you sell
Insurance policies Until renewed or replaced + 1 year Proof of coverage if a claim question comes up later
Insurance claim paperwork Until the claim fully closes + 1 year Claims can be reopened
Health insurance statements (EOBs) Until you've checked them, then toss or shred Your provider keeps the details; keep only disputed ones
Medical bills and receipts 1–3 years, longer if tax-deductible or in dispute Matches tax rules and dispute windows
Bank statements 1 year (7 if they support taxes) Banks keep records; your tax rules are stricter
Credit card statements 1 year (7 if they support taxes) Same logic as bank statements
Pay stubs Until they match your W-2 or year-end statement Then the W-2 is the record
Utility bills Until payment clears; 1 year if needed for home office or rental claims Rarely needed beyond that
Rent lease + move-in inspection Duration of tenancy + a few years (varies) Deposit disputes can surface later; check your local rules
Mortgage statements Life of the loan + 3 years Loan payoff disputes happen
Loan payoff letters ("satisfaction" letters) Forever Proof the debt is gone
Vehicle title and bill of sale While you own the car + a few years Needed to sell; proof of ownership history
Vehicle maintenance records While you own the car Boosts resale price
Warranties and big purchase receipts Life of the product That's what a warranty is
Household inventory / photos for insurance Contents swap, keep current version Makes claims painless
IDs, passports, birth/death/marriage certificates Forever (paper original) Replacing them is misery
Estate documents (wills, trusts, POA — current versions) Forever (current); shred superseded drafts Obviates "which one is real?" disasters
Receipts for everyday purchases Until the return window closes Then the card statement has it
Retirement account statements Year-end statements; 7 years for tax-linked ones Custodians keep the rest
Old photos and sentimental paper Your call — no guilt rule applies Photograph what matters, keep a few originals
Instruction manuals Life of the appliance (or find PDFs online) Free up a whole drawer
School records, diplomas, transcripts Forever Verification comes up surprisingly often

Common questions, answered plainly

"Why 7 years for taxes if the IRS says 3?" Rules change, states differ, and "substantial understatement" extends the window to 6. Seven years absorbs all of those with room to spare. Storage is free once it's a PDF.

"Can I get rid of paper tax returns after scanning?" Mostly yes — the IRS accepts digital records. Exception worth honoring: anyone whose situation involves estate property, amended filings, or unusual forms may want the paper return kept indefinitely. When unsure, keeping is cheap.

"What about 'keep forever' items that already got scanned?" The scan is for convenience; the paper original of certificates, deeds, and titles is the legally convenient one. Keep both.

"A drawer I forgot about is full of 2009 bank statements." If they don't support a tax year you're still keeping: shredding rules apply. This page plus ten minutes is the fix.

A one-line sticky note version

Taxes 7 · Insurance until replaced · Certificates forever · Bills until paid · If in doubt, scan it and keep the PDF.
