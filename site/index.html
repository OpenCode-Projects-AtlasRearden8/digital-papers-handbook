Paper doesn't have to rule your life. This handbook shows you, step by step, how to tame it — like a friend would explain it, never condescending.

Who this is for

This handbook is for anyone who has a drawer, a box, or a shoebox full of papers and no idea what any of it means. It's written to be low-energy and ADHD-friendly: each chapter is short, each step is small, and nothing requires a weekend you don't have.

You don't need apps, subscriptions, or special gear. You need your phone, a folder on your computer, and about an hour a year.

The three rules this handbook is built on

  1. Local-first. Your files live on your computer, not trapped in someone else's cloud. Cloud syncing is fine as a backup, but you should always be able to work without it.
  2. Simple beats clever. A folder system you actually use beats a filing system that would impress a librarian.
  3. Small beats perfect. Ten minutes today is worth more than an ambitious plan for Saturday.

How to use this handbook

Read Keep or Toss and Folders and Naming first — together they take about 15 minutes and cover 80% of the value. Everything else is reference material for when you need it.

Then put a one-hour "paper cleanup" on your calendar for some quiet day this month. That's it. That's the whole commitment.
